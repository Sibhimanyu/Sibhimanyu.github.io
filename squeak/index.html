03 / The duck

Quiet until it matters.

A rubber duck sits in a corner of your screen and in the menu bar. Click it for the list.

  • Working

    A quick bob while any agent is mid-turn.

  • Needs you

    Wide eyes and a red badge. Something is blocked until you act.

  • Done

    Celebrates for a short while after a turn really ends.

  • Asleep

    Nothing running. The duck rests.

04 / Away from the desk

05 / Trust and compatibility

Your Mac stays in charge.

Answering an agent means pressing keys in another app's window, so Squeak proves each step before it types.

  • Only approved phones

    A phone joins after you approve it on the Mac.

  • The right session

    Squeak checks which chat or tab is in front before it types.

  • Delivery is checked

    A reply is confirmed in the chat, and never sent twice.

How each check works, and what Squeak can answer

  • The Mac trusts a key, not a server

    Each phone reply is signed by the paired phone, expires after ten minutes, and can answer only the chat's current question.

  • The agent must be in front

    Squeak reads which chat is on screen from Conductor's own route, never from a tab label. In Terminal, the agent must own the tab and show its own empty prompt, so text never lands in a shell or over your draft.

  • Confirmed, never retyped

    A reply that may have gone is checked against the chat's history. If Squeak cannot confirm it, it says so.

06 / Install

Keep your agents in view.

Squeak 2.1.11 for Mac, as a disk image. It is free and ad-hoc signed rather than notarized, so macOS asks once before the first launch. After that, Squeak updates itself.

Download for Mac All releases

Squeak-2.1.11.dmg · macOS 14 or later · Free

  1. 1

    Open the disk image

    Open Squeak-2.1.11.dmg from your Downloads and drag Squeak onto Applications.

  2. 2

    Open it the first time

    Right-click Squeak in Applications, choose Open, then Open again. If macOS still refuses, press Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

  3. 3

    Find it in the menu bar

    Squeak lists your Conductor chats on its own. Answering them also needs the Squeak service running on the Mac.
